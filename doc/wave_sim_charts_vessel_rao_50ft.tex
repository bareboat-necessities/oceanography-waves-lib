\maketitle
These charts show vessel motion, rather than surface-particle motion, for an
estimated 50-foot fin-keel sailboat at zero forward speed with fixed heading
and an IMU at the center of gravity. Waterline length is 12.500 m, beam 5.179 m,
and draft 2.679 m. Natural periods for heave, pitch, and roll are 3.207, 3.742,
and 4.677 seconds, with damping ratios 0.45, 0.35, and 0.22.

The hull dimensions scale from the 28-foot preset by 50/28; all response
periods and horizontal time constants scale by the square root of that ratio.
Damping ratios are held fixed. This assumes geometric similarity, not a
particular production hull or loading condition.

Every motion plot includes all four incident-height cases: 0.27, 1.5, 4.0, and 8.5 m.
The first 60 seconds of each 20-minute, nominal 200 Hz record are plotted at
20 Hz. World acceleration excludes gravity; accelerometer specific force
includes gravity. Rotations and gyro readings describe the vessel rigid body.
These are analytical surrogate RAOs, not measured hull data. The largest sea
states extrapolate beyond the small-motion regime and are stress tests.
The CSV filenames continue to describe the incident wave parameters.

The complete dataset is \texttt{sim-data-files-vessel-rao-50ft.zip}; its internal
filenames and columns match the surface dataset. See \texttt{doc/vessel-rao.md}
for the response equations, coordinate conventions, and regular-wave details.

\section{Applied displacement RAOs}
These curves sample the production C++ transfer function with deep-water
$k=\omega^2/g$, from 0.005 to 1 Hz. Translation gains are m/m and rotation
gains are rad/m of incident elevation amplitude. Wave propagation is relative
to the bow: 0 degrees following, 90 beam, and 180 head waves. These are
transfer functions, not motion spectra; regular bound harmonics may use a
different wavenumber. Positive phase is a lag under $\exp(-i\omega t)$.
Phase is wrapped to $[-180,180]$ degrees and omitted for zero response.
Yaw is constrained to zero. The heading maps show gain, not phase.
